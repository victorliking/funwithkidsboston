When we first started exploring Boston with our little ones, finding the right place to stay felt overwhelming. After years of trial and error (and some pretty memorable hotel disasters!), I've learned that knowing where to stay in Boston with kids can make or break your family vacation. The good news? Boston is surprisingly family-friendly, and there are some fantastic hotels that truly get what families need.

Whether you're visiting from out of town or looking for a staycation spot right here in Greater Boston, I've stayed at most of these places with my own kids over the years. From hotels with pools that saved us during those brutal February weeks to spots with kitchenettes that made breakfast prep a breeze, here are my tried-and-true recommendations for the best family hotels in Boston.

## Getting There & Getting Around Boston

Before diving into specific hotels, let's talk logistics. Boston's compact size is perfect for families – you can walk to most attractions, and the T makes getting around pretty easy. However, driving and parking can be a nightmare, especially in Back Bay and downtown. If you're flying in, Logan Airport is incredibly convenient, with Silver Line connections and hotel shuttles that make the journey with car seats and strollers much more manageable.

**Good to Know:**
- Most hotels charge $35-50/night for parking
- Many offer package deals that include attraction tickets
- Book early for summer and fall foliage season
- Always ask about connecting rooms for larger families

## Downtown Boston: Best for First-Time Visitors

### Boston Harbor Hotel
**Address:** 70 Rowes Wharf, Boston

This is where luxury meets family-friendly, and honestly, it's worth the splurge if it's in your budget. The kids were mesmerized by the water taxi that docks right at the hotel – it felt like our own private yacht! The location is unbeatable: walking distance to the Children's Museum, Faneuil Hall, and the Freedom Trail.

The hotel provides cribs, pack-and-plays, and even has a kids' welcome amenity that had our toddler squealing with delight. The concierge team knows their stuff about family activities and always has great restaurant recommendations that actually welcome kids with open arms.

Pro tip: Book a harbor view room. Yes, it's pricier, but watching the sunrise over Boston Harbor while your kids are still sleeping is pure magic. Plus, the planes coming and going from Logan provide endless entertainment for little airplane enthusiasts.

**Good to Know:**
- Rates: $400-800/night depending on season
- Valet parking: $52/night
- Indoor pool and fitness center
- Concierge can arrange babysitting services

### Omni Parker House
**Address:** 60 School Street, Boston

This historic hotel (seriously, it's been around since 1855!) has that classic Boston charm, but don't worry – they've updated everything for modern families. The location on the Freedom Trail is perfect for history-loving kids, and you're literally steps from Boston Common and the Public Garden.

We love the spacious rooms and the fact that they provide rollaway beds and cribs at no extra charge. The hotel restaurant, Parker's, is surprisingly kid-friendly, and their Boston cream pie is legendary (yes, it was invented here!). Fair warning: the rooms can be on the smaller side, so if you have multiple kids, definitely ask about connecting rooms.

**Good to Know:**
- Rates: $200-450/night
- Self-parking: $42/night (valet available)
- Pet-friendly (great if you're bringing Fido)
- Historic charm with modern amenities

## Back Bay: Perfect for Shopping and Strolling

### The Colonnade Hotel
**Address:** 120 Huntington Avenue, Boston

Here's where I always recommend families stay when they want to be in the heart of everything. The Colonnade sits perfectly between Back Bay and the South End, putting you within walking distance of Newbury Street shopping (great for picking up souvenirs), the Prudential Center, and some of Boston's best family restaurants.

What sets this place apart? The seasonal rooftop pool! Open Memorial Day through Labor Day, it's the only rooftop pool in the city. Our kids still talk about swimming while looking out over the Boston skyline. Just heads up – it can get busy on weekends, so morning pool time is your best bet.

The rooms are spacious by Boston standards, and many have separate sitting areas that work perfectly as kid zones. The staff has always been incredibly accommodating when we've needed extra towels, late check-out, or recommendations for kid-friendly dinner spots nearby.

**Good to Know:**
- Rates: $250-500/night
- Valet parking: $49/night
- Seasonal rooftop pool (huge hit with kids!)
- Near Hynes Convention Center T stop (Green Line)

### Fairmont Copley Plaza
**Address:** 138 St. James Avenue, Boston

This grand dame of Boston hotels might seem intimidating with kids, but they've embraced families beautifully. The lobby is stunning – think marble floors and crystal chandeliers – but the staff doesn't bat an eye when little ones are running around (within reason, of course).

The location can't be beat: you're across from Trinity Church, steps from Copley Square, and the Boston Public Library (which has an amazing children's section) is right there. We've made it a tradition to grab hot chocolate from the lobby cafe and walk through the library's beautiful halls.

One thing I absolutely love: they offer a "Family Fun" package that includes welcome gifts for kids, discounted breakfast, and late checkout. It's these little touches that make traveling with children so much easier.

**Good to Know:**
- Rates: $300-600/night
- Valet parking: $52/night
- Copley T stop (Green Line) right outside
- Elegant atmosphere that still welcomes families

## Cambridge: Great for Longer Stays

### Residence Inn Boston Cambridge
**Address:** 6 Cambridge Center, Cambridge

If you're planning a longer stay or have multiple kids, this is your spot. Every room is a suite with a full kitchen, separate living area, and workspace – basically like having your own apartment. The kitchens have saved us so much money on dining out, and having space to spread out makes everyone happier.

The location in Kendall Square means you're close to MIT (great for science-loving kids), and the Red Line takes you directly into downtown Boston in about 10 minutes. There's a small playground right across the street at the Cambridge Center, which has been a lifesaver for burning off energy.

The complimentary breakfast is actually good – not just continental breakfast sad pastries. They have a waffle station that the kids love, and the staff will pack breakfast to-go if you're heading out early for adventures.

**Good to Know:**
- Rates: $200-400/night
- Self-parking: $25/night (much cheaper than downtown!)
- Full kitchens in every suite
- Kendall/MIT T stop nearby (Red Line)

### Hotel Veritas
**Address:** 1 Remington Street, Cambridge

This boutique hotel near Harvard Square is perfect for families who want something unique but still kid-friendly. The Harvard Square location means you're surrounded by street performers, bookstores, and casual restaurants that welcome families.

The rooms are cozy (read: small by suburban standards), but they're thoughtfully designed. Many have window seats that kids love, and the bathrooms have great water pressure – important when you're dealing with sandy kids after a day at nearby Fresh Pond.

What really won me over was how the staff remembered our kids' names and preferences from our first stay. They even kept extra juice boxes at the front desk because they knew our youngest was particular about his morning routine.

**Good to Know:**
- Rates: $180-350/night
- Street parking available (free on Sundays!)
- Harvard Square T stop 2 blocks away (Red Line)
- Boutique charm with personal service

## Nearby Activities and Day Trips

### Seaport District Options
**Address:** Various locations along the waterfront

The Seaport has exploded with family-friendly hotels over the past few years. The Element Boston Seaport and Aloft Boston Seaport are both excellent choices, especially if you want to be near the Boston Children's Museum, Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum, and the Harbor Islands ferry.

The neighborhood feels newer and less crowded than downtown, with wide sidewalks perfect for strollers and plenty of green space. Plus, parking tends to be slightly easier (though still expensive) than in the historic core.

## Family Hotel Extras That Matter

After staying at dozens of Boston hotels with kids, here are the amenities that actually make a difference:

**Must-Haves:**
- Refrigerator (for snacks, milk, and leftovers)
- Connecting rooms or suites with separate spaces
- Cribs and rollaway beds at no extra charge
- 24-hour room service or nearby late-night food options

**Nice-to-Haves:**
- Pool (indoor is better for unpredictable New England weather)
- Laundry facilities (especially for longer stays)
- Kids' welcome amenities
- Babysitting services or kid-friendly room service menus

**Nearby:** Most of these hotels are within walking distance of Boston Common, where you'll find the famous Swan Boats and Frog Pond (ice skating in winter, wading in summer).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the best neighborhood to stay in Boston with young kids?

For first-time visitors, I always recommend Back Bay or downtown. You'll be walking distance to most major attractions, and the T connections are excellent. If you're planning a longer stay or want more space, Cambridge offers great value with easy access to downtown via the Red Line.

### How much should I expect to pay for a family hotel in Boston?

Budget $200-300/night for decent family accommodations, with luxury options running $400-800/night. Summer and fall foliage season (September-October) are most expensive. January-March typically offers the best deals, though you'll be dealing with our brutal New England winters.

### Do Boston hotels provide cribs and rollaway beds?

Most major hotels provide pack-and-plays or cribs free of charge, but always call ahead to reserve them. Rollaway beds usually cost $20-40/night extra. Pro tip: bring your own fitted crib sheet – hotel-provided bedding doesn't always fit properly.

### What about parking at Boston hotels?

Parking is expensive ($25-52/night) and sometimes limited. If you're planning to use public transportation and walk to attractions, consider hotels near T stops and skip the rental car entirely. Many hotels offer package deals that include parking discounts.

### Are there any hotels with pools suitable for kids?

The Colonnade's rooftop pool is the most unique, but it's seasonal. Most Marriott and Hilton properties have indoor pools year-round. Always check pool hours – many close early (around 9 PM) and some have restricted children's hours.
